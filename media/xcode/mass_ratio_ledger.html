What survives a reduced ion mass ratio

Measured across a 73× range in ion mass — mp/me = 25, 100, 400 and real — on one laser-ablation problem in WarpX. Every exponent below is fitted to four runs, not assumed from the algebra, and all four now carry the corrected initial corona drift.

in 1D
Don’t reduce.

Real mass is 3.7 h on one RTX 4070 — 39× the m_p/m_e = 100 leg. The reduction saves under four hours and costs you the absorbed fraction.

in 2D
You have no choice.

Cost goes as μ² instead of μ1.5, so the same run at real mass is ~1300 h. The question becomes which convention, not whether.

The ledger

μ = mi,leg / mi,real. Exponents are least-squares fits in log μ over the four legs; scatter is the residual spread.

μ0exact
Every numerical parameterpreserved

dz = 8.467e−08 m, dt = 9.885e−17 s, ω_pe·dt = 0.783, dz/λ_D = 58.1 — bit-identical in all four legs. They are set by the electron, which no leg touches. Reducing the ion mass buys nothing in resolution; the entire saving is fewer cells × fewer steps.

μ07% scatter
Te against its own steady statepreserved

T_e / (823·μ^(1/3)) = 0.580 / 0.506 / 0.547 / 0.535 across the sweep — a 1.15× spread, inside the 13.5 % noise floor. This is the quantity to compare, never raw eV.

μ0.322theory ⅓
Plume Te in raw eVrescales

113.9 / 157.7 / 271.0 / 440.2 eV. Manheimer’s steady state carries μ^(1/3), and the measured 0.322 sits 3.3 % from it over a 73× mass range, with 4.5 % scatter. Extrapolating the reduced legs to real mass predicts 416 eV against 440 measured — 5.8 %, inside the 13.5 % noise floor.

μ0.490theory 0
Optical depth, and therefore absorptionbroken

The similarity transform is supposed to leave ∫K dz invariant: K ∝ T^(−3/2) falls as μ^(−1/2) exactly as the path L ∝ d_i0 grows as μ^(1/2). It does not, because that cancellation needs T scaled by μ^(1/3) too. Pin the handoff in raw eV instead and K stops moving, leaving τ_abs ∝ μ^(1/2). Measured 0.490 — 2 % from the μ^(1/2) that branch predicts, with 4.6 % scatter, so the failure is fully accounted for by the convention. fabs ran 0.205 → 0.840 across the sweep.

μ1/6analytic
Collisionality relative to scaleirreducible

λ_ei ∝ T²/(n lnΛ) is mass-independent, while L ∝ μ^(1/2). So λ_ei/L ∝ μ^(1/6) — at m_p/m_e = 100 the plasma is 1.62× more collisional relative to its own scale than the real one. No handoff convention fixes this; only more mass does.

μ−1/2a trap
Every velocity in the initial conditioneasy to miss

Holding v/C_S0 fixed means the physical velocity scales as μ^(−1/2). The run READMEs list drift_uz_de under “held fixed by design” and it is neither an s¹ length nor an s² time. Held fixed, the real-mass leg started its corona 4.29× too fast and opened a density notch propagating at 2.28 C_S0.

The fork you cannot avoid

Both branches are defensible. They answer different questions, and mixing them is what produced the μ0.490 above.

Hand off the real state, in raw eV

The initial condition is the rad-hydro state. Temperatures are directly comparable to FLASH in eV, which is what a cross-code benchmark needs.

Optical depth goes as μ^(1/2) (measured 0.490). At m_p/m_e = 100 the leg absorbs 4.2× less than the real problem, and no post-hoc f_abs^(2/3) correction repairs it — applied across the sweep that correction makes the scatter worse, not better.

What this project did. Right for benchmarking, wrong for physics at low μ.

Transfer everything in similarity units

Scale the handoff temperature by μ^(1/3) as well, and ∫K dz is genuinely invariant. Absorption, T_e/T_ss and the flow all transfer.

The initial condition is no longer the state any other code computed, so raw-eV comparison to FLASH becomes meaningless. You are simulating the reduced-mass analogue of the problem, not the problem.

Right when the reduced run must stand on its own — a 2D campaign, a parameter sweep.

The one thing you cannot do is mix them. Normalised lengths and times with a raw-eV temperature is exactly the combination that suppresses κ by μ^(−1/2) and under-drives the run. If the temperature is not on the same footing as the lengths, the transform is not a transform.

Cost measured, not modelled: 58.4 s / 345 s / 2009 s / 12 910 s on one RTX 4070, fitting μ^1.258 against an ideal μ^1.5 — the GPU is underused below ~10k cells, so treat 1.258 as a floor. In 2D the transverse extent scales too and the exponent becomes μ².

If you do reduce, the recipe

  1. s¹ on every length quoted in d_e — target thickness, centre, corona scale length and offset, domain bounds, max_grid_size. s = √(mr/2698).
  2. s² on times and step counts — mass_ratio, max_step, the laser profile interval and all four diagnostic intervals.
  3. 1/s on velocities, 1/s² on velocity ramps quoted per d_e. This family is missing from the run READMEs and is the one that bit us.
  4. μ1/3 on the handoff temperature — only if you took the similarity branch above. Skipping it is what breaks the optical depth.
  5. Leave dz, dt, cfl, ppc and λ_D alone. They belong to the electron and are already right.
  6. Check three things before believing the run: uza/C_S0 against the rad-hydro corona’s 0.548, and ζ_cr and L_n against it too. The temperature is not a sensitive tell — the bad leg’s T_e was off by 5 % while its L_n was off by 4×.
  7. Keep the domain divisible by blocking_factor 8 and reset max_grid_size to match, or a 1D GPU run loses 7.9×.
